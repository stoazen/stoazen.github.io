# Eight Themes

Before you draw, pick the trouble closest to what you're feeling right now. One quote can belong to several themes. For example, the Stoic idea of what is and isn't in your control speaks to both anxiety and hard choices. Each theme has at least 15 quotes.

## Anxiety

A restless mind, too much worry about the future.

> We suffer more often in imagination than in reality.
>
> — Seneca, *Letters to Lucilius* 13

## Anger & Conflict

Feeling provoked, friction with other people.

> The best way of avenging thyself is not to become like the wrong doer.
>
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 6.6

## Action & Inertia

You know what you should do, but you can't get moving.

> Nothing is so fatiguing as the eternal hanging on of an uncompleted task.
>
> — William James, *The Principles of Psychology*

## Loss & Setback

Failure, rejection, loss.

> When we can no longer change a situation, we are called on to change ourselves.
>
> — Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning*

## Choices & Doubt

Not knowing which way to go.

> The Great Way is not difficult. It only dislikes picking and choosing.
>
> — Sengcan, *Faith in Mind* (Xinxin Ming)

## Relationships

Disappointed expectations, boundaries, comparing yourself to others.

> What is most personal is most general.
>
> — Carl Rogers, *On Becoming a Person*

## Self-Doubt

Feeling you're not good enough.

> The curious paradox is that when I accept myself just as I am, then I can change.
>
> — Carl Rogers, *On Becoming a Person*

## Letting Go

Holding on to what can't be changed.

> Dreams, illusions, flowers in the sky: why trouble yourself trying to grasp them?
>
> — Sengcan, *Faith in Mind* (Xinxin Ming)

## Whatever Comes

When nothing in particular is troubling you, choose Whatever Comes. It doesn't filter by theme. It draws one card from all 180 quotes, so you can see what today brings.
