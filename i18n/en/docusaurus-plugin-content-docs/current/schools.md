# Three Schools

StoaZen holds 180 quotes: 60 each from Zen, Stoic philosophy and psychology. The school is just a label on the card and doesn't affect the draw. For the same trouble, you might get an answer from any of the three.

## Zen

Zen doesn't explain things. It pulls you back to what's in front of you. Many Zen sayings are only a few words long. At first they don't seem to answer the question at all, yet they loosen the question itself.

> It is not the wind moving, and not the flag moving. It is your mind that moves.
>
> — Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch, *The Platform Sutra*

**Main sources**: *The Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch*, *The Gateless Gate* (Wumenguan), *The Blue Cliff Record*, *Faith in Mind* (Xinxin Ming), *Song of Enlightenment* (Zhengdao Ge), *The Record of Linji*, *The Jingde Record of the Transmission of the Lamp*, *The Recorded Sayings of Layman Pang*

**Figures included**: Huineng the Sixth Patriarch, Zhaozhou Congshen, Sengcan, Yongjia Xuanjue, Linji Yixuan, Layman Pang, Mazu Daoyi, Yunmen Wenyan, and others

## Stoic philosophy

At the heart of Stoicism is one distinction: what is within my control, and what is not. We can't choose what happens to us, but we can choose how we judge it and how we respond.

> Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things.
>
> — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 5

**Main sources**: *Meditations*, *Enchiridion* (Handbook), *Letters to Lucilius* (Moral Letters), *On the Shortness of Life*, *On Anger*

**Figures included**: Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus, Seneca

## Psychology

Modern psychology draws on observation and clinical experience to talk about how people face loss, build habits and accept themselves. It fills in what the ancient traditions touch on less: the workings behind our emotions and behavior.

> When we can no longer change a situation, we are called on to change ourselves.
>
> — Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning*

**Main sources**: *Man's Search for Meaning*, *The Principles of Psychology*, *On Becoming a Person*, *Understanding Human Nature*, *Toward a Psychology of Being*, *Playing and Reality*, and others

**Figures included**: William James, Viktor Frankl, Sigmund Freud, Carl Rogers, Alfred Adler, Abraham Maslow, Carl Jung, Donald Winnicott, and others
